Why this matters now

The CRA doesn't stop when you ship. Neither should your security process.

Under the Cyber Resilience Act, handling vulnerabilities is a legal obligation for the whole support period of a product. An SBOM at release is only the start.

Vulnerability handling

A new CVE just dropped. Now what?

sbomify links the finding to the products and releases that ship it, so your team goes from an alert to a recorded decision, a tracked fix and a published advisory.

  1. Which of your supported releases contain it?Three of four, straight from their SBOMs
  2. Are you actually affected?v2.0 and v2.1 are; v1.2.1 is not, recorded with VEX
  3. Does it need a patch?Fixed in 2.0.4, in progress for 2.1
  4. What do you tell your customers?An advisory, on your Trust Center and as CSAF

Less time reconstructing your software history. More time fixing what matters. Vulnerability scanning →

AI-enabled, not AI-powered

Your agents. Your data. Your decisions.

Your AI agents already know your codebase. sbomify gives them structured security data and an API built for them, with a token that can propose work but never approve it.

  • Investigate a CVEin the context of your own code, using the triage and fix prompts sbomify generates
  • Propose VEX statementsthat explain why a release is or isn't affected
  • Draft security advisoriesand propose updates to published ones

A person signs off. VEX statements from an agent wait in a review queue, and its changes to published advisories are shown to your team as a diff. sbomify doesn't bundle an AI model, so you choose which agents and model providers to use, and where your data goes.
