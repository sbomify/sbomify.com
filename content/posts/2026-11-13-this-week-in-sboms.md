This is the fourth entry in **This Week in SBOMs**, reviewing events from **Saturday 7 to Friday 13 November 2026**. [Last week](/2026/11/06/this-week-in-sboms-2026-11-06/) we learned that a version string alone does not determine exposure; it was the build process itself. This week, the build system became the target twice, including one incident involving valid provenance.

Regulators largely adjusted their timelines. Standards organisations issued significant documentation, some of which will disrupt your parsing logic.

## The big one: stolen GitHub accounts push a CI secret stealer

Beginning on **10 November at 12:45 UTC**, 4,837 compromised GitHub accounts submitted probe pull requests titled `docs: dependency audit notes` across 61,764 repositories, based on data from [Aikido](https://www.aikido.dev/blog/compromised-github-accounts-push-ci-secret-stealer-to-tens-of-thousands-of-repositories). Where the account possessed collaborator or organisation permissions, it deployed a workflow designed to harvest credentials. Aikido identifies at least 1,806 repositories where the payload executed and cautions that this "may understate impact" because the attacker removes the execution logs. [SafeDep](https://safedep.io/blog/github-actions-wfx-credential-stealer) recorded 8,093 repositories with malicious commits between 10 November and 12 November: 7,309 workflow injections and 771 npm `postinstall` hooks, which also execute on developer workstations.

The payload extracts Actions secrets, `GITHUB_TOKEN`, OIDC tokens, cloud credential files, and keys for AI providers. It exchanges OIDC tokens for AWS, GCP and Azure sessions and establishes persistence that endures a key rotation: AWS role trust policies are altered to `"Principal": {"AWS": "*"}`, and `wfx-ci-*` federated credentials are inserted into Entra applications. Subsequently, it reverts the commit and removes the workflow run.

Most of the harm concerns credentials, though some incidents affected packages. Aikido and SafeDep identify Packagist `digitalelvis/neuronai-studio` 3.14.3, Go `github.com/onuragtas/docker-env` v1.0.278 and v1.0.280, and a `github.com/vkuznet/gocurl` pseudo-version. SafeDep also lists docker-env v1.0.282 and `github.com/onuragtas/redock`. The compromised accounts overlap with the October GhostAction wave, but Aikido states "we don't attribute November to the same actor at this stage". How the accounts were breached remains undisclosed. ([THN](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/4837-hijacked-github-accounts-ran-ci.html), [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-compromise-4-800-github-accounts-to-steal-cloud-credentials/))

**Why this matters for SBOM/VEX practitioners:** your SBOM documents which dependency version was deployed. It does not indicate whether the maintainer's CI environment was secure at the time of that build. For the specific packages named, the precise version allows you to distinguish safe from unsafe releases; query your SBOMs for these versions immediately. Go pseudo-versions highlight why the complete version string is critical, not merely the module path. For your own pipelines, verify cloud trust policies and federated credentials, as rotating secrets does not sever an established trust relationship.

## Security news that mattered

**Ethersphere bee-js: a malicious release with valid provenance (9 November).** [Socket](https://socket.dev/blog/bee-js-compromise) reports that `@ethersphere/bee-js` 13.3.0 "was published by Ethersphere's legitimate trusted-publishing workflow with valid SLSA provenance", containing a `postinstall` loader. npm removed it after approximately 75 minutes. Swarm Desktop 0.56.2, which was signed and notarized, was distributed via the auto-updater. The safe release is [0.56.3](https://github.com/ethersphere/swarm-desktop/releases/tag/v0.56.3), and the maintainer notes 0.56.2 "was published by an attacker using stolen CI credentials". ([GHSA-hh7j-5m3j-23pf](https://github.com/advisories/GHSA-hh7j-5m3j-23pf), [THN](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-compromise-etherspheres-bee-js.html)) _Why it matters:_ provenance identifies the pipeline that constructed an artifact, not whether that pipeline was under proper control. Regard it as a single element of evidence, not as an endorsement.

**SSHole is in KEV and being exploited (9 November).** CISA [added](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/alerts/2026/11/09/cisa-adds-four-known-exploited-vulnerabilities-catalog) OpenSSH [CVE-2026-123158](https://www.cve.org/CVERecord?id=CVE-2026-123158), last week's musl password bypass, with a due date of 12 November. THN cites GreyNoise seeing the first attempt on 7 November. On oss-security, Medcrypt [reproduced it](https://www.openwall.com/lists/oss-security/2026/11/09/27) "on a stock Yocto image, no unusual configuration", and musl's maintainer [said](https://www.openwall.com/lists/oss-security/2026/11/09/28) `crypt()` "will possibly be changed (to always return null for failure) in the next release cycle". OE-core backported 10.6p2 to scarthgap on 10 November ([commit](https://github.com/openembedded/openembedded-core/commit/fc4316861becc9f0d41857b49f6438929ab4c291)); we found no Buildroot backport to its 2026.08.x or 2025.02.x branches as of 13 November. ([THN](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/cisa-warns-of-active-exploitation-of.html), [BleepingComputer](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/hackers-exploit-sshole-openssh-flaw-to-breach-linux-systems/), [Schneier](https://www.schneier.com/blog/archives/2026/11/new-openssh-vulnerability.html)) _Why it matters:_ embedded Linux builds are where musl and no-PAM setups are common and where SBOMs are thinnest. If you ship Yocto or Buildroot images, the SBOM is how you answer customers.

**PHP2Shell reaches Check Point gateways, and a post-mortem on why scanners missed it (10 to 12 November).** [Check Point Research](https://research.checkpoint.com/2026/php2shell-exploitation-on-check-point-gateway/) reports exploitation since 30 October against Mobile Access portals, "consistent with PHP2Shell, associated with CVE-2026-109618 and CVE-2026-109619", chained with a Gaia privilege escalation, CVE-2026-123039. The [GVIP project's write-up](https://www.gvip-project.org/blog/2026/php2shell/) is the SBOM story: official Docker images compile PHP into `/usr/local`, so "No dpkg, apk or rpm entry owns it", and "Trivy did catch it in an image that shipped an SBOM naming its PHP runtime." ([THN](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/hackers-exploit-php2shell-flaws-in.html)) _Why it matters:_ this confirms [what we wrote two weeks ago](/2026/10/30/this-week-in-sboms-2026-10-30/). A runtime built from source shows up in an SBOM only if someone writes it down.

**Toolchain and runtime releases:**

- **Go 1.27.4 and 1.26.11 (10 November)** fix 12 CVEs ([announcement](https://www.openwall.com/lists/oss-security/2026/11/10/28)). Eleven, CVE-2026-97033 to CVE-2026-97043, are in cmd/go, cgo, the compiler and the linker, where "building a malicious package that uses cgo could cause arbitrary code execution at build time". _Why it matters:_ a dependency can run code when you build it, not only when you run it. Record the Go toolchain version in build provenance.
- **Node.js 22.23.5, 24.22.3 and 26.13.1 (11 November)** fix 16 CVEs ([advisory](https://nodejs.org/en/blog/vulnerability/november-2026-security-releases)). One, CVE-2026-123864, can leak "TLS private keys and other users' requests". 20.x and 25.x are end-of-life and get no fixes.
- **Git 2.56.1 (10 November)**, backported to 2.50.2 through 2.55.1, fixes six CVEs ([oss-security](https://www.openwall.com/lists/oss-security/2026/11/11/10), [GitHub](https://github.blog/open-source/git/git-security-vulnerabilities-announced-7/)). The worst, CVE-2026-111760, allows code execution on `git clone --recurse-submodules`. "All Git versions back to v2.27.0 are affected." CI images and products that bundle Git need checking, not just laptops.
- **Apache httpd 2.4.71 (11 November)** fixes a critical mod_http2 bug, [CVE-2026-119618](https://httpd.apache.org/security/vulnerabilities_24.html#CVE-2026-119618), in 2.4.55 through 2.4.70 and standalone mod_h2 2.0.8 through 2.0.42. The standalone module is archived and has no fixed release. THN reports no known exploitation. ([THN](https://thehackernews.com/2026/11/critical-apache-http2-flaw-could-let.html), [HN](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=50462177))
- **Also on oss-security:** PostgreSQL 18.7 and back-branches fix 52 CVEs, and 14 is now end-of-life ([release](https://www.postgresql.org/about/news/postgresql-187-1712-1616-1520-1425-and-19-rc-2-released-3442/)). MIT krb5 1.22.3 fixes 21 ([post](https://www.openwall.com/lists/oss-security/2026/11/09/38)). OpenSSL merged five low-severity fixes and scheduled releases for 1 December ([post](https://www.openwall.com/lists/oss-security/2026/11/10/12)).

**KEV.** Ten additions in the window, all on 9 and 10 November. CISA flags three as affecting an open-source component: OpenSSH CVE-2026-123158, GPUStack CVE-2026-102895 and FreeSWITCH CVE-2026-116779, all due 12 November. Chromium CVE-2026-125350, a use-after-free in "the XML component", is due 24 November. Reports that it sits in libxml2 are unconfirmed. See our [KEV explainer](/2025/12/30/what-is-kev-cisa-known-exploited-vulnerabilities/).

**Last week's watch list.** On 9 November, Apple [published a note](https://support.apple.com/en-us/149555) stating that "starting in macOS 27.2.1" the command-line utilities will cease to trust the eMudhra certificates linked to the compromise, offering equivalent safeguards in macOS 26.7.3 and 15.8.3. Go and Node.js were released as previously stated. The Brotli KEV deadline expired on 10 November without altering the entry's status. Docker Hardened Images issued an advisory on 10 November assigning a VEX status of `under_investigation` to its OpenSSH images; this represents the initial image-vendor VEX declaration concerning SSHole we have located.

## CRA

**The Commission adjusts the standardisation request (10 November).** The Commission's [CRA standardisation page](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-standardisation) indicates the request "was amended ... to extend the deadline for the delivery of all vertical standards and some horizontal standards by 3 months", pursuant to decision C(2026)7861. Neither specific horizontal standards nor revised dates are identified, and access to the decision document itself was unavailable. _Why it matters:_ the harmonised standards required to operationalise the CRA's SBOM and vulnerability-handling obligations are now delayed relative to the original schedule, while the regulation's application dates remain untouched. For now, alignment should be planned against the [regulation text](/compliance/eu-cra/) and the ENISA SBOM advisory rather than a standard that has not yet been finalised.

**EN 40000-1-1 and 1-2.** No primary confirmation exists as to whether either standard was ratified. CEN-CENELEC has issued no statement, and neither title appears in the Official Journal.

**Germany: the CRA act is now item 7.** The Bundesrat's [agenda for 20 November](https://www.bundesrat.de/SharedDocs/TO/1070/tagesordnung-1070.html), dated 13 November, lists the implementation act (Drucksache 621/26) as item 7 rather than item 6. The legislation designates the BSI as the market surveillance authority, making it responsible for inspecting SBOMs and vulnerability handling within Germany.

**ENISA.** The [SRP page](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/topics/product-security/vulnerability-services/eu-incident-response-and-cyber-crisis-management/single-reporting-platform-srp) continues to state that the v1.3 documentation "is currently being updated". ENISA's [State of Cybersecurity in the Union report](https://www.enisa.europa.eu/news/enisa-reveals-the-state-of-cybersecurity-in-the-union) (10 November) notes that the count of nations with a national coordinated vulnerability disclosure policy increased "from 7 to 17" since 2024. Coordinated disclosure is the prerequisite step for VEX workflows.

**Eclipse ORC.** The FAQ task force's [10 November minutes](https://github.com/orcwg/orcwg/commits/main) document the CRA FAQ expanding from 72 to 129 entries, with steward inquiries largely triaged and manufacturer questions pending review. ORC's [monthly post](https://orcwg.org/blog/orc-monthly-oct-2026/) highlights that ENISA's consultation regarding security.txt, CVD policies, and security advisories concludes on 15 November. If you hold views on [security.txt](/faq/what-is-security-txt/), this weekend marks the deadline.

## NIS2

**France: debate concluded, vote scheduled for 17 November.** The Assemblée nationale [says](https://www.assemblee-nationale.fr/dyn/actualites-accueil-hub/examen-du-projet-de-loi-relatif-a-la-resilience-des-infrastructures-critiques-et-au-renforcement-de-la-cybersecurite) it reviewed the bill transposing NIS2, CER and DORA on 9 and 10 November. The formal vote is set for "mardi 17 novembre après les Questions au Gouvernement". The Senate adopted its version in March 2025, so a joint committee may still be needed to reconcile the two texts.

Otherwise quiet: no infringement decisions were issued, and there were no updates on the Digital Omnibus or the Cybersecurity Act revision. The Council press room failed to load for us. Background information is available on our [NIS2 page](/compliance/eu-nis2/).

## CISA and US regulators

It was a quiet week. CISA's sole press release concerned a revised [incident reporting form](https://www.cisa.gov/news-events/news/cisa-announces-enhanced-reporting-form-cyber-incidents) aligned with MITRE ATT&CK, which omitted any reference to SBOMs. Searches of the Federal Register for "software bill of materials", SBOM, "cryptographic inventory", "post-quantum" and FASCSA yielded no results within the period. We found no new developments regarding the [CISA 2026 Minimum Elements](/compliance/cisa-minimum-elements/), the SSDF revision or the FCC bills-of-materials proposal.

## Other regulators

**UK: a government department requires an AIBOM (10 November).** The Department for Work and Pensions [published](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/dwp-procurement-security-policies-and-standards) Security Standard SS-040 on AI and machine learning. The [PDF](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/6af5d060cc0dbfec767a50bd/standard-artificial-intelligence-and-machine-learning-version-1.pdf) is dated 29 October. Clause 11.7.2 says: "System owners must maintain an up-to-date AI Bill of Materials (AIBOM), Machine Learning Bill of Materials (ML-BOM), or equivalent formally governed assurance asset record." The minimum contents include models, datasets, prompt templates, tool connectors, MCP servers, licences, provenance and hashes. Another clause requires license-policy pipelines to "fail closed" before merging into protected branches. _Relevance:_ this is procurement policy, so it applies to DWP suppliers, and the field list goes well beyond what most AI-BOM tooling produces today.

**UK Bill.** The Cyber Security and Resilience Bill's next step is Commons consideration of Lords amendments, listed for 24 November ([Parliament](https://bills.parliament.uk/bills/4035)). It has not had Royal Assent.

Nothing new from BSI on TR-03183, METI, CERT-In or Singapore's CSA. MAS's quantum-safe consultation, with its proposed [CBOM](/faq/what-is-a-cbom/)-style inventory, closes on 23 November. Australia's ASD site did not load for us.

## Standards

**Ecma TC54 publishes four sets of minutes (12 November).** The [15 October minutes](https://github.com/Ecma-TC54/meetings/pull/15) record consensus on a first batch of CycloneDX 2.0 changes. These include pre-defined perspectives (the examples are CISA minimum elements, BSI minimum elements and the CRA SBOM expert group), EPSS, risk alignment and a rename of `bom-ref` to `refId`. The minutes call the rename "one of the few breaking changes from CycloneDX 1.7". They justify EPSS on the grounds that "CycloneDX is now an API format instead of a file format". Earlier minutes, merged the same day, record that TEA, the second edition of PURL (ECMA-427) and VERS were approved for submission to the December Ecma General Assembly. CycloneDX 2.0 was expected to be released through OWASP in November and submitted to Ecma in June 2027. The 12 November agenda added "Concerns raised at JTC 1 plenary", including "Normative referencing SPDX". _Why it matters:_ anything that parses `bom-ref` will need changing for 2.0. Separately, PURL, VERS and TEA are a month away from becoming Ecma standards.

**CycloneDX.** Still 1.7.2, with no 2.0 release candidate. Thirteen PRs landed on `2.0-dev`, including [#1090](https://github.com/CycloneDX/specification/pull/1090), which removes `metadata.supplier` in favour of `metadata.component.supplier`. [cdxgen 13.7.0](https://github.com/cdxgen/cdxgen/releases/tag/v13.7.0) can already write 2.0 "against the pinned draft schema" with `--spec-version 2.0`, and adds a validation rule set for the CISA 2026 minimum elements.

**PURL.** [purl-spec v1.1.1](https://github.com/package-url/purl-spec/releases/tag/v1.1.1) (10 November) contains "changes required to complete the review cycle for ECMA-427 2nd Edition". It also pins down PyPI name normalisation: lowercase, and collapse runs of `.`, `_` and `-` into a single hyphen. If your tool produced `pkg:pypi/Foo_Bar`, the same package will not deduplicate against tools that follow the spec. The Go casing PR [#1054](https://github.com/package-url/purl-spec/pull/1054) is still open.

**SPDX.** Still 3.1 RC1, but RC2 content is landing in [spdx-3-model](https://github.com/spdx/spdx-3-model): AI agent support (#1257), a hardware model number (#1441) and project fields (#1461). On 7 November the same author proposed recording evidence for dependency edges in both SPDX ([#1483](https://github.com/spdx/spdx-3-model/issues/1483)) and CycloneDX ([#1197](https://github.com/CycloneDX/specification/issues/1197)).

## The wider landscape

- **OpenSSF:** The Supply Chain Integrity working group charter vote passed the 55% threshold on 10 November, with 5 out of 9 votes ([#682](https://github.com/ossf/tac/issues/682)). The issue is still formally open. ORT's maintainer has [requested](https://github.com/ossf/tac/issues/686) that the TAC consider transferring the OSS Review Toolkit from the Linux Foundation's ACT programme to OpenSSF, calling ACT "pretty much dead".
- **CycloneDX tooling:** The [cyclonedx-node-npm 7.0.0](https://github.com/CycloneDX/cyclonedx-node-npm/releases/tag/v7.0.0) update now sets spec 1.7 as default and ceases to install dependencies required for validation or XML generation. cdxgen 12.9.2 incorporates two security patches, while cargo-cyclonedx 0.5.10 enables the production of CycloneDX 1.6 and 1.7 formats from Rust projects.
- **Dependency-Track:** No new release occurred; however, PR [#7942](https://github.com/DependencyTrack/dependency-track/pull/7942) rectifies pre-1.7 CycloneDX exports that previously failed schema validation when components contained license expressions.
- **Research:** Our search found no arXiv papers regarding SBOMs or VEX submitted during the reporting window. Submissions dated 12 and 13 November may yet be pending indexing.
